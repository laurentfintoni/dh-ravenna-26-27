# 86145 - DIGITAL HUMANITIES / 48693 - INFORMATICA PER I BENI CULTURALI / B5079 - DIGITAL HUMANITIES E DATA MANAGEMENT PER I BENI CULTURALI 
## AA 2026-2027

### Learning Outcomes 

The course aims to introduce students to the field of Digital Humanities and the inter-disciplinary common methodological outlook it offers. Students can expect to learn about how to develop, design, create, and manage digital resources for different humanities fields and purposes using a group project-based learning approach that blends theory and practice. In particular, the course will introduce methodologies, techniques, and standards useful for enhancing cultural heritage in its many forms. At the end of the course, students will be able to apply the knowledge acquired to represent and manage different types of cultural heritage data in a humanities context and to produce, manage, and publish digital resources related to cultural heritage in accordance with the principles of Open Science.

### Course Contents

The course is intended as a cross-disciplinary introduction to the field of Digital Humanities (DH), and in particular how the DH offer a common methodological outlook that is well suited to enhancing cultural heritage in its many forms. 

Topics covered will include: research & data literacy (how to find, evaluate, and work with information), metadata & data management (how to describe and work with information), data manipulation & presentation (how to analyze and present information), and key scientific, legal, and ethical frameworks (how, when and why you should share your work) such as Open Science, and the FAIR and CARE principles.  

The course will follow a project-based learning approach that blends theory and practice of the models, tools, and principles necessary to conceive, design, and implement a digital resource related to cultural heritage. Students can expect to be introduced to key theoretical groundings that have driven and continue to drive the field of DH as well as practical ways to work with cultural heritage in a digital context.

### Teaching methods

The course, with a total duration of 30 hours, will aim to enhance students' learning potential with various approaches and tools: 

- Project-based learning to allow students to actively explore and confront the topics and themes of the course. In particular, the final exam will be project-based and students are expected to work in groups that include members of the different programmes. 

- Lessons will be recorded and made available for download as a support for reviewing content, whether the students are attending or non-attending. The recording of lessons by students for their own specific needs can be considered but must be discussed and agreed to prior to the beginning of the course. 

- Slides and all course materials will also be made available to consult at any time, again as a support for reviewing content. Whenever possible additional material will be made available to deepen topics covered during the lessons.  

- Participatory exercises (such as interactive q&a sessions) will be used to allow the class as a whole to discuss what they are learning and present practical and theoretical implications. 

- Collaborative practical learning activities will balance the theoretical elements of the course to give students a chance to work together to integrate theory and practice and to develop skills useful for the final exam. These activities will also be a primary vehicle for introducing potential tools that can be used for the final exam and which contextualize the topics of the course. 

- The use of Generative AI and LLMs is permitted but subject to strict guidelines (see Teaching Tools). The non-use of Generative AI and LLMs is  encouraged. The choice of either approach and its application will be part of the final evaluation and grade.  

### Assessment methods

The course will be assessed via a project-based final exam, intended to evaluate students' knowledge of and ability to implement both theoretical and practical elements introduced during the course. 

The exact details of what this final project will require, how it will be evaluated, and the grade assignment will be confirmed before the course starts at the end of October 2026. It will, broadly speaking, involve the development of a digital resource related to cultural heritage that must then be presented during an oral exam. 

Specific guidelines for the project will be given during the course. 

Students are expected to work on the project as a group, which should include students from the different programmes. Exact details of group sizes and possible exceptions will be given before the course begins. 

Each group member's individual contribution and understanding of the course topics will be assessed during the oral exam. 

The use or non-use of Generative AI and LLMs in the completion of the final project will be part of the final evaluation and grade. 

### Teaching tools

The lessons take place in a classroom equipped with personal computers connected to the intranet and the internet. 

Students who have their own personal computers or similar devices (such as tablets) are encouraged to bring them to the classroom. 

Theoretical lessons will always be accompanied by practical sessions.

All course materials – including lecture notes and slides – will be made available on Virtuale and online via GitHub. 

Students who, due to disabilities or specific learning disorders (SLD), require compensatory tools are encouraged to inform the instructor of their needs so they can be directed to the appropriate contacts and arrangements can be made to adopt the most suitable measures. For more information: https://site.unibo.it/studenti-con-disabilita-e-dsa/en/for-students

The use of Generative AI and LLMs, by which is intended agents, chatbots, and other interfaces where text prompts can be used to produce an output, for the final project is permitted but will be subject to strict and specific guidelines. The use of GenAI and LLMs during the course for educational support is also permitted, per the current guidelines of the University of Bologna. Details of the course's guidelines for the use of GenAI and LLMs will be made available by the end of October 2026. 

Equally, the non-use of Generative AI and LLMs is encouraged. Students must decide if they want to use these technologies and how. The potential implications of these choices will be discussed in the first class alongside the guidelines, final exam, and other essential contextual elements of the course.
